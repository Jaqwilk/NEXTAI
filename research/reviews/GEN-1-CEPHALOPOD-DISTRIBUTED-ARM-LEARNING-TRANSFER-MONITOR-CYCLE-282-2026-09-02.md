## Objective observations

### `SRC-0417` and `SRC-0418`: cross-side tactile transfer is genuine and central

Blinded octopuses learned rough-versus-smooth object discrimination using arms
on one side. Intact animals responded correctly when the same objects were
presented through the untrained side. A longitudinal split of the
supraoesophageal brain preserved learning on the trained side but abolished
correct response through the opposite side. Further ipsilateral lesions showed
that touch learning remained possible until the corresponding subfrontal lobe
was completely removed.

This satisfies the behavioral core of naive-side transfer and supplies a strong
causal intervention. It contradicts the desired locus, however. Transfer depends
on communication between bilateral central tactile-memory systems. The arms are
interchangeable sensory-effectors for a central learned discrimination; the
experiments do not show an experience-written arm-cord state moving between
arms.

### `SRC-0419`: the central brain reads focal arm information

Octopuses learned two-choice single-arm mazes using arm position or tactile
cues while vision of the arm and target was excluded. Performance was not tied
to one particular arm. This demonstrates proprioceptive and tactile information
flow from a focal arm to central control.

It does not establish naive-arm transfer. Participating arms receive task
experience, and there is no first unrewarded trial of an untouched arm, arm-cord
plasticity intervention or central-versus-peripheral storage dissociation.

### `SRC-0420` and `SRC-0421`: sophisticated peripheral movement is prewired

Mechanical or electrical stimulation elicited normal-like extension from arms
disconnected from the brain. Separate tract experiments found broad en passant
recruitment of motor pools, consistent with global central commands being
combined with local sensory state rather than a centrally labeled command for
every arm position.

These findings genuinely reduce central control complexity, but they test
evoked motor organization rather than learning. The direct software analogue is
a fixed dynamic movement primitive or distributed plant controller, explicitly
excluded by the frozen contract.

### `SRC-0422`: peripheral interarm communication exists

In preparations containing arms and the interbrachial nerve ring but no brain,
touching one arm evoked graded activity in its axial cord, the ring and other
arms, including across the midline. Signal probability and strength declined
with arm distance; cutting the intervening commissure abolished downstream
transmission.

This is the strongest architectural ingredient for a distributed system, but
the transmitted quantity is an immediate mechanosensory signal. There is no
training, retention, transfer of learned state, task behavior or change in the
route with experience.

### `SRC-0423`: peripheral plasticity is suggested, not localized

Dwarf cuttlefish reduced strikes at prey enclosed in a transparent tube over
training and retained the reduction four days later. Training was associated
with increased phosphorylated CREB in sucker and epithelial-fold sensory tissue,
especially in distal arm regions.

The experiment does not restrict learning to an identified arm, and all arms
may contact the tube. pCREB is an activity/plasticity correlate rather than a
causal memory manipulation. No naive-arm probe, transfer, lesion, permutation or
scaling test distinguishes a local learned trace from peripheral sensing that
supports central learning.

## Cross-source synthesis

The literature establishes three separate facts: tactile memories can transfer
between sides, arms contain embedded motor programs, and peripheral nerve-ring
connections transmit signals between arms. It does not join these facts into a
single experience-written distributed learner. The experiment that actually
tests transfer localizes its necessity to intact bilateral central circuitry.
The experiments that isolate peripheral computation contain no learning, and
the molecular plasticity observation contains no arm-specific causal transfer.

The smallest sufficient account is therefore a central tactile memory that can
be queried through multiple arms, broadcasting commands into fixed peripheral
motor programs while immediate sensory signals also travel through the nerve
ring. This is capable and biologically unusual, but its software translation is
central associative memory plus reusable motor primitives and routing—not the
portable local learner required by NEXTAI.
