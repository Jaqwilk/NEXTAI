## Objective observations

### `SRC-0396` and `SRC-0397`: real structure learning, but inside authored bases

Variable practice over rotations or shearings changes initial behavior,
reduces interference and biases exploration when a new transform belongs to
the experienced family. Position- versus velocity-dependent force fields also
change both feedback corrections and later feedforward adaptation. These are
genuine learning-to-learn effects, including behavior before extensive target
retraining.

The family structure is nevertheless supplied by the experiment: horizontal
or vertical rotation, shearing, and position- or velocity-dependent force.
Every task shares the same reaching apparatus, coordinates, effector and error
semantics. The observed effect is exactly a learned low-dimensional prior over
control parameters. A hierarchical Bayesian model, basis-restricted adaptive
filter or structured state-space learner implements that account directly.
Neither study intervenes on cerebellar plasticity or identifies one local rule
across a qualitatively different family.

### `SRC-0398`, `SRC-0399` and `SRC-0406`: adaptation and transfer dissociate

Cerebellar stimulation accelerated right-hand visuomotor adaptation in one
study but did not alter transfer to the untrained hand, even when training was
shortened to separate acquired magnitude from plateau exposure. A larger
seven-experiment study found no consistent stimulation effect across modest
changes in display, timing, tool and perturbation schedule.

Lesion mapping independently associates force-field and visuomotor adaptation
with adjacent but only partly overlapping cerebellar regions; patient
performance in the two tasks does not correlate. Thus cerebellar involvement
in acquisition is causal or clinically supported, but it does not establish a
single portable update or readout.

### `SRC-0400`–`SRC-0403`: error-driven models remain task-local

Removing online correction while retaining sensory prediction error preserves
healthy adaptation, whereas hereditary cerebellar ataxia impairs it. In a
force-field task, cerebellar-degeneration patients still change commands, but
the changes are not informed by the preceding error. These findings isolate
prediction error as a cerebellum-dependent teaching signal.

They do not identify a new computational operation. Error-driven latent-state
update is the standard state-space, LMS/RLS/Kalman and adaptive-control class.
The acquired rotated-mouse fMRI signature is correlational, and Purkinje-cell
recordings under viscous and elastic loads emphasize kinematics rather than a
single inverse force model. No source tests first-read transfer between
unaligned task families or reports a three-scale full-cost crossover.

### `SRC-0404`, `SRC-0405` and `SRC-0407`: causal cross-modal savings is not immediate transfer

Tone–shock conditioning accelerates later light–shock conditioning and vice
versa. Unpaired exposure does not. Cerebellar inactivation during either stage
prevents the savings, local interpositus plasticity has an associative
signature, and interpositus activity changes before much of the pontine change.
This is the strongest factor-specific result in the audit.

It fails the decisive temporal boundary. The second sensory stimulus does not
immediately elicit the learned blink; it must be repeatedly paired with the
same periorbital US. Both modalities converge on the same authored outcome,
timing and cerebellar output circuit. The result is accelerated acquisition of
a new address to a retained response, not a learned predictor transferred to
an unseen computation before target feedback.

## Cross-source synthesis

The literature supports three reusable effects: a learned prior over a supplied
low-dimensional perturbation family, an error-driven task-local adaptive state,
and savings when a new sensory address is trained onto the same conditioned
output. Each is real and biologically important. Together they still do not
provide one source-identical local rule across qualitatively different
families. The direct software translations are structured Bayesian/adaptive
control, basis-function generalization and retained-state reacquisition.

The frozen gate therefore receives no trigger. A software experiment would
either rediscover a classical adaptive filter with a supplied feature basis or
test a cache/savings effect already covered by earlier NEXTAI controls.
