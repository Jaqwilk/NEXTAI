# GEN-1 breadth portfolio and literature review — cycle 171

## Scope

The scoring part of this cycle completed exactly one experiment,
`EXP-20260901-0029`. That result brought the durable count from 74 to 75 and
triggered the configured six-result literature cadence. This review closes the
same wake, as cycle 158 did, without another plan, seed, candidate mutation,
benchmark mutation or runner call. It reviews the six results since completed
count 69: `EXP-20260901-0020`, `0021`, `0022`, `0024`, `0027` and `0029`.

## Objective portfolio observation

- `0020`: tree-reassociated Born-MPS contraction preserved predictions and
  reduced declared dependency-depth slope, but increased total query work and
  lost quality/cost to unigram.
- `0021`: learned persistent macros were exact and saved 0.58% of warm query
  work at depth 16, but input traversal and learning made R16 work about 81%
  higher than exact interpretation.
- `0022`: sparse active-cone scheduling changed K slope from about one to zero
  without changing output, while the learned quadratic representation lost
  NRMSE, retention and full cost to affine ridge.
- `0024`: future-supervised predictive state improved its source-identical H96
  ablations, but a simple public-control residual bank was markedly more
  accurate, cheaper and Pareto-superior.
- `0027`: learned factor energy decreased monotonically, yet iterative
  relaxation worsened byte likelihood relative to one sweep and lost sharply
  to unigram/PPM.
- `0029`: surprise gating removed about 45% of reveal-update work and improved
  accuracy versus a dense clock, but frozen transitions beat its loss in all 45
  paired trials and PPM/CTW were much stronger.

Across six mechanisms, scheduling, contraction, persistence, future supervision
or event selection repeatedly produced a genuine component signature. None
earned the learned representation that supplied it: a simpler classical or
frozen control recovered equal or better capability at lower full cost. This
supports HYP-0012 as accounting discipline but must not increase its confidence
or generate another confirmation series.

## Prior-art boundary

`SRC-0059`, `SRC-0171` and `SRC-0196` already cover event-triggered estimation,
surprise-updated neural memory and predictive local plasticity. They make the
update saving in `0029` unsurprising and require trigger computation to be
charged. `SRC-0108` explains why PPM-D's variable-order context is the relevant
classical sequence null. `SRC-0090` shows that e-graphs and anti-unification
already learn abstractions modulo rewrite equivalence, while `0021` directly
showed exact learned macros failing full-cost amortization. Therefore the
bidirectional equational-rewrite direction proposed conditionally in the
`0029` analysis is observationally redundant and is rejected before a protected
migration. `SRC-0005` leaves one materially different dormant question open:
a compact learned controller with external memory or an instruction language,
but historical NEXTAI toy VM results failed finite-search and extrapolation
controls.

## Portfolio decision

Do not tune or combine the six failed rules. Keep their scheduler-level
signatures only as controls. Reject an equational-rewrite v6 cohort because
BABBLE/e-graph prior art and the exact interpreter/macro result already supply
the discriminating null. Do not raise HYP-0012 confidence.

The next wake may perform exactly one no-scoring feasibility-and-contract cycle
for `repository_bytecode_superinstruction_contract_v1`, a conditional HYP-0009
revival grounded in real local repository programs rather than another hand-
generated algebra. Before any protected activation it must establish, from
tracked local files only, all of the following:

1. deterministic whole-module train/development/test hashes and evaluator-only
   disassembly into anonymous opcode, operand-class and stack-effect traces;
2. a capability target that requires exact held-out execution or state
   transformation, not trace compression, next-op prediction or supplied
   outputs;
3. at least three genuine program-length or memory scales and extrapolation
   beyond training length;
4. one unchanged controller/instruction learner without paths, names, constants,
   source text, family labels, external models or manual instruction ontology;
5. exact bytecode interpretation, bounded finite search, Sequitur/BPE-style
   superinstructions, profile-guided basic-block caching and supplied-program VM
   controls with acquisition, tracing, compilation, dispatch, memory, update and
   R1/R4/R16 costs;
6. a proof that a learned macro cannot win merely by copying evaluator-provided
   trace segments or by moving full interpretation into preprocessing.

If an exact capability boundary cannot be frozen with the existing repository
without a hand-authored VM or target leakage, record
`no_repository_bytecode_superinstruction_contract` and stop this branch in that
same service wake. If it passes, freeze the smallest cohort with no hypothesis,
candidate, plan, seed or scoring. The immediately following wake must
preregister and score one quick; it may not spend another audit cycle. A valid
negative ends the exact learned-instruction route without tuning.

## Confidence and limits

Confidence is high that the six exact rules failed their preregistered gates;
each is one-seed screening evidence and does not prove a universal lower bound.
The proposed bytecode gate is deliberately uncertain: it is selected because it
changes the computational object from passive prediction to executable learned
instructions, not because success is expected. No general successor claim is
supported.
