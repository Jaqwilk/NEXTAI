## Objective observations

### `SRC-0378`–`SRC-0380`: causal mobile state, but a fixed broadcast

Grafted mosaic tobacco separates systemic signal generation and perception:
SABP2 methyl-salicylate esterase activity is needed in distant tissue. In
Arabidopsis, FMO1 produces N-hydroxypipecolic acid (NHP); externally supplied
NHP moves systemically and rescues an `fmo1` SAR defect. These are unusually
clean causal demonstrations of local-to-global state propagation.

NHP is induced by bacterial, fungal and oomycete attack across several plant
species. It can protect Brachypodium against both Xanthomonas and Magnaporthe.
That breadth is the strongest apparent transfer result in this audit. Yet
application of the same metabolite without a training pathogen reproduces the
effect. The signal therefore carries a fixed danger/defense mode, not a mapping
learned from observations of one pathogen and applied to another.

### `SRC-0381` and `SRC-0382`: descendant breadth is shared-axis sensitization

Repeated, fitness-reducing Pseudomonas infection produced Arabidopsis
descendants with stronger SA-responsive gene induction and better resistance
to both Pseudomonas and the oomycete Hyaloperonospora. A separate study found a
similar descendant phenotype after either avirulent Pseudomonas or chemical
BABA priming. Thus cross-taxonomic protection is real in the reported samples
and cannot be dismissed as same-strain recall.

The discriminating interventions identify a common explanation. The phenotype
requires NPR1, primes SA-dependent PR genes, associates with permissive
chromatin, is mimicked by non-CpG hypomethylation and is reproducible by a
generic chemical primer. It also suppresses JA-dependent defense and increases
susceptibility to the necrotroph Alternaria. This is an inherited bias toward
one pre-existing immune mode with a trade-off, not accurate discovery of an
operator that generalizes between unfamiliar pathogen families.

The effect lasts only one unexposed generation in these experiments. Repeated
severe parental infection was required for the longer report, and the infected
parents suffered dramatic growth and seed-production costs. Neither paper
jointly supplies three spatial or generational scales, reset, matched full cost
and separation from chromatin memory.

### `SRC-0383`: direct replication uncertainty is material

A later direct reassessment repeatedly infected parents by two delivery methods
and at different developmental stages. It found no descendant improvement in
bacterial resistance, defense-gene induction or the tested chromatin signal.
This does not erase the positive papers, but establishes substantial
protocol-dependent uncertainty and prevents treating transgenerational SAR as
a robust reusable primitive.

### `SRC-0384` and `SRC-0385`: the correct classical control is priming

Constitutively active SAR mutations reduce fitness in multiple environments.
Low-dose chemical priming incurs less growth and seed cost than constitutive or
direct defense while producing nearly comparable protection after challenge.
Accordingly, the mandatory computational baseline is a cheap persistent alarm
or lowered response threshold, not a dense model. None of the plant studies
beats that simpler account at matched useful quality and full end-to-end cost.

## Cross-source synthesis

The evidence separates into three mechanisms:

- fixed mobile danger signals that broadcast a global immune mode;
- temporary sensitization of the conserved SA/NPR1/PR response;
- epigenetic persistence of that response threshold into descendants.

All three are biologically useful and some are genuinely local, incremental
and low-cost relative to direct defense. None learns pathogen-specific content
that improves a truly unseen family without using the same terminal immune
axis. The Alternaria reversal is especially discriminating: apparent broad
transfer is a mode trade-off, not a portable representation. No source passes
the frozen write/transport/read, cross-family, three-scale and full-cost gate.
