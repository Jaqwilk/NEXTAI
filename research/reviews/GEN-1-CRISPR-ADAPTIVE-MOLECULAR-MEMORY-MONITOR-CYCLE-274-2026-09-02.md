## Objective observations

### `SRC-0352`: direct spacer acquisition and sequence-specific resistance

Barrangou et al. provide the foundational causal result. Viral challenge of
*Streptococcus thermophilus* produces new phage-derived spacers; adding a spacer
creates resistance and removing it removes resistance. Resistance follows
spacer-phage sequence similarity.

This is genuine observation-written, heritable and locally appendable molecular
memory. It does not pass the prospective gate because its read operation is a
sequence address. The paper does not show experience with one family improving
defense against an unrelated family without a matching address, nor a
three-scale full-cost crossover.

### `SRC-0353` and `SRC-0354`: primed adaptation is related-sequence local search

Datsenko et al. show that an imperfectly matching crRNA that no longer provides
ordinary interference can prime rapid acquisition of additional spacers from
the same foreign DNA. The effect requires the matching protospacer and CRISPR
acquisition/interference components and helps counter mutants of a previously
encountered virus.

Fineran et al. show that priming tolerates extensive PAM and protospacer
degeneracy. Their nominally unrelated plasmid nevertheless contains a sequence
with up to seven mismatches to the priming spacer, and new spacers are acquired
from that target molecule. These are strong demonstrations of robust local
search around a partially matching address, not abstraction across unrelated
families.

### `SRC-0355`: functional target selection and competing resistance

Heler et al. isolate parts of the write/read pathway. Cas1, Cas2 and Csn2 are
needed to acquire new spacers but not to execute immunity from an existing
spacer; Cas9 and PAM recognition bias acquisition toward targets that Cas9 can
later cleave. The experiment also observes phage-resistant survivors with
envelope resistance.

This supports a carefully gated sequence-target acquisition mechanism and
underscores the need for matched receptor controls. It does not show a learned
representation that transfers beyond sequence compatibility.

### `SRC-0356`: single-cell timing exposes stochastic population filtering

McKenzie et al. use microfluidics and time-lapse microscopy to separate direct
interference from primed adaptation. Direct interference acts fairly uniformly
within about one to three generations. Primed adaptation varies from about two
to thirty generations, with rapid protection in only a subpopulation.

The single-cell evidence is valuable because it prevents population survival
from being mislabeled as uniform within-lineage computation. The system uses an
engineered target plasmid and still supplies no unrelated-family transfer.

### `SRC-0357`: diversity distributes exact addresses across a population

Van Houte et al. assemble populations with increasing numbers of clones, each
carrying a distinct phage-targeting spacer. Greater spacer diversity suppresses
viral escape more effectively. The breadth is therefore an ensemble effect:
many exact addresses distributed across a population make simultaneous escape
harder.

This is useful population-level adaptation, but it is not one lineage learning
a portable operator. The diversity and targets are supplied by the experimental
population construction.

## Cross-source synthesis

The audited CRISPR mechanisms form three established computational classes:

- append-only sequence-addressed memory (`SRC-0352`);
- mismatch-tolerant local search and renewed sampling around a related address
  (`SRC-0353`, `SRC-0354`, `SRC-0355`);
- stochastic acquisition followed by population filtering and diversity
  (`SRC-0356`, `SRC-0357`).

No source demonstrates unrelated-family transfer without shared protospacer
information. No source jointly supplies acquisition-off and interference-off
causal isolation, same-lineage cross-family improvement, three scales and full
end-to-end cost. Combining sequence acquisition from one paper, priming from
another and population diversity from a third would not establish one portable
learner.

## Interpretation and confidence

property for NEXTAI is representational abstraction: the memory is a biological
address book, priming explores nearby sequence space, and broad defense emerges
from accumulating addresses or distributing them across a population.
