## Objective observations

### `SRC-0408`: first-test cross-modal relation transfer is real

Honeybees learned delayed matching-to-sample and nonmatching-to-sample in a
Y-maze. With novel stimuli and no reward during transfer, colour-trained bees
matched gratings, grating-trained bees matched colours, and odour-trained bees
matched colours. Matching choice was around 65–76% in the reported visual
transfers. The rewarded arm changed throughout training and transfer tests
placed the matching choice on both sides, so a fixed left/right response does
not explain the result.

This is the strongest anomaly in the audit. It still misses the frozen causal
boundary. The bees received 60–80 rewarded visits in the same binary
sample-then-choice protocol. In two experiments, including odour-to-colour,
the future transfer stimuli were sometimes shown during unrewarded
familiarization. There was one binary relation depth, no mushroom-body or
identified-circuit manipulation, no acquisition-versus-expression separation
and no end-to-end cost comparison.

Most importantly, the observed computation need not be a learned symbolic
relation. Every trial asks whether the currently offered stimulus repeats the
sample. A fixed sensory-accommodation or novelty signal can therefore be
associated with approach for matching and avoidance for nonmatching while
generalizing to unseen stimulus identities.

### `SRC-0409` and `SRC-0410`: visual relations combine, but axes are supplied

Honeybees generalized above/below relations after distance and absolute
location varied. A later experiment combined spatial relation with element
difference and transferred the conjunction to novel visual displays. These
controls are stronger than memorizing particular pictures and demonstrate
flexible relational behavior.

Both studies keep one visual Y-maze, two-element displays and experimenter-
defined above/below or left/right axes. They do not test a qualitatively new
modality, an unknown relation depth, a local learning rule or a causal circuit
write/read boundary. The direct software comparator is a fixed spatial
relation extractor followed by associative choice.

### `SRC-0411` and `SRC-0412`: cross-modal pattern transfer is aligned and selective

Bumblebees learned circular versus cross-shaped scent arrangements and later
preferred novel unscented visual flowers with the corresponding arrangement,
without additional learning. The modalities differ, but the experimenter
provides a pointwise spatial correspondence: the same circle or cross exists
in scent and pigment coordinates.

The boundary is not universal. Bumblebees that learned temperature patterns
failed to prefer corresponding visual patterns, and visual learning did not
transfer to temperature, even though each modality was detectable and
learnable. The positive scent-to-vision result is therefore compatible with
modality-pair-specific spatial feature binding rather than one source-identical
abstract update.

### `SRC-0413`: object recognition crosses touch and vision

Bumblebees trained for 33 trials to discriminate rewarded cubes from spheres
by touch or vision preferred the same object in an unrewarded test through the
other modality. Modality isolation controls were strong, and transfer worked
in both directions.

The target is the identity of the same cube or sphere, not a relation among
independently permuted objects. The authors explicitly leave unresolved
whether whole shape or local curved-versus-flat features support the result.
No circuit intervention, novel-object permutation, relational depth or cost
scaling isolates a portable learned operator.

### `SRC-0414` and `SRC-0415`: causal mushroom-body evidence stops at association

Reversible mushroom-body blockade during conditioning impaired honeybee
olfactory positive-patterning acquisition while sparing elemental
discrimination. In Drosophila, matched visual and olfactory assays showed that
the same dopamine neurons and partially overlapping Kenyon-cell subsets are
required for memories in both modalities.

These are valuable causal interventions, but they do not connect to the
sameness/difference transfer experiment. Configural learning remains
olfactory, and the Drosophila work forms separate visual and olfactory
associations under a shared reinforcement circuit. A shared anatomical center
does not establish shared learned content or first-test relation transfer.

### `SRC-0416`: a simple repetition comparator reproduces the headline behavior

A mushroom-body-inspired network reproduces honeybee matching and nonmatching
learning and transfer. Sensory accommodation makes repeated stimuli evoke a
different magnitude from novel stimuli; an associative output path learns to
approach or avoid that signal according to reward. The authors explicitly
describe the supported concept learning as limited.

This model is not causal biological evidence and its fitted constants are not
a full-cost baseline. It is nevertheless a concrete alternative explanation:
the behavioral anomaly does not require the animal to discover and store a
portable symbolic equality operator. A fixed comparator plus ordinary reward
plasticity is sufficient in the published task structure.

## Cross-source synthesis

The sources establish three genuine abilities: unrewarded transfer of binary
matching to new stimuli and from odour to colour, cross-modal retrieval of an
aligned spatial pattern or object shape, and mushroom-body dependence of
configural association. No single experiment joins them. The relational
experiments lack causal circuit isolation; the circuit experiments lack
relation transfer; positive cross-modal cases preserve a common geometric or
object property and fail for another learnable modality pair.

The simplest unified account is not a new general operator. It is a set of
fixed comparators or modality bridges feeding a shared reinforcement system:
repetition/novelty for delayed matching, spatial alignment for scent-to-vision,
and shape features for touch-to-vision. This account is implementable with
classical similarity/configural association and would be the mandatory null
for any future test.
