This is one bounded no-scoring SEARCH MODE cycle in a conceptual neighborhood
distinct from physical update rules and endogenous intervention. The gate in

## Audited observations

### Human collective foraging

`SRC-0317` measured 128 participants foraging alone and in groups of four in
structured or random virtual environments. Individual success drove adaptive
asocial search and selective attention to successful peers. After controlling
for resource depletion, however, the social condition had no reliable effect
on aggregate reward (`0.002`, 95% HPDI `[-0.02, 0.02]`), and performance did
not improve reliably over rounds. The authors explicitly report no evidence of
meta-learning. The strongest recent direct experiment therefore supplies a
null group-level gain, while its visible success events and rewards remain
authored semantics.

### Cumulative pigeon-route culture

`SRC-0318` is the strongest anomaly. Chains of homing-pigeon pairs with staged
member replacement improved route efficiency beyond solo birds and fixed
pairs, while route similarity persisted across generations. This rules out a
pure static many-wrongs average. The proposed mechanism nevertheless combines
individual route innovations, social transmission and pruning by route payoff.
The precise transmission rule is left unresolved. One species, one navigation
task, no factor-specific algorithmic rule ablation and no common full-cost
boundary cannot establish a source-identical learner.

### Biological and artificial fish

`SRC-0319` shows that artificial fish trained from pixels by deep RL with
curiosity-derived rewards develop grouping and social preferences resembling
observations in zebrafish. This demonstrates sufficiency of generic rewarded
learning for one behavior family, not identity with the biological mechanism.
The biological fish are not shown to execute the ANN algorithm, curiosity is
an explicit preference signal, and reduced curiosity weakens the artificial
effect. Parallel behavior therefore does not supply the missing causal rule.

### Physical robot swarm

`SRC-0320` reports physical 3–12 robot formations and an approximately `34%`
per-robot power reduction for distributed versus centralized sensing. The
system is engineered around a master broadcaster, labeled ArUco markers,
custom TDMA packets, predefined formations, consensus control, PID, Kalman
filtering and covariance fusion. It is valuable systems evidence, but no local
interaction rule is learned from observations.

## Observation

Collective systems can improve by pooling complementary samples, carrying
state across members or generations, selecting innovations using observed
payoff, or executing fixed communication and control protocols. The only
audited case beyond static averaging—the pigeon succession experiment—does not
identify the executable rule, test it across an unrelated system or separate
social transmission from payoff-filtered route memory. No source adds a new
information-bearing edge to the cycle-261 DAG.

## Interpretation, uncertainty and confidence

Multiplicity changes where observations, storage and computation reside, but
does not create target-useful information ex nihilo. Under an auditable
boundary, group gains map to more observations, communication, external state,
selection credit or a fixed protocol. A collective benchmark would encode one
of those channels and would not test a new causal thesis.

Confidence is `0.995` that none of `SRC-0317` through `SRC-0320` passes the
prospective gate, `0.99` that the physical swarm uses fixed classical
protocols, and `0.98` that biological/artificial fish behavior does not prove
rule identity. Confidence is `0.94` that the pigeon result is not currently
sufficient for a software mechanism, because its transmission law remains
unidentified. Confidence is only `0.68` that no future collective experiment
will isolate a portable learned interaction rule.
