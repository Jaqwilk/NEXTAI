# Epigenetic/chromatin memory monitor — cycle 271

## Scope and frozen question

This SEARCH MODE cycle performed no hypothesis creation, plan creation, seed
realization, candidate implementation, scoring or protected-apparatus change.
The prospective contract was frozen before source inspection in
`research/checks/epigenetic_chromatin_memory_monitor_cycle_271.json`, with the

The question was deliberately stronger than whether biological trained immunity
exists. It asked whether one primary factor-specific experiment jointly shows an
exposure-written, source-identical local epigenetic rule that transfers across
unrelated families and divisions after genetics, selection, persistent stimulus,
composition, growth and metabolism are matched, at three scales and full cost.

## Objective observations

### `SRC-0337`: monocyte training after Candida or beta-glucan

Quintin et al. demonstrate T/B-cell-independent, monocyte-dependent protection
against Candida reinfection and persistent enhanced human-monocyte responses to
several unrelated microbial ligands. Dectin-1/Raf-1 and histone-methylation
interventions implicate H3K4me3-associated reprogramming. The primary in-vivo
outcome is nevertheless same-pathogen reinfection; the heterologous outcomes are
broad cytokine gains, and chromatin is not isolated from metabolic or persistent
effector state.

### `SRC-0338`: heterologous human responses after BCG

Kleinnijenhuis et al. report increased responses to unrelated bacterial and
fungal pathogens for at least three months after BCG vaccination. NOD2 dependence
and increased H3K4me3 at inflammatory promoters support a causal training pathway.
The observable remains nonspecific inflammatory gain rather than a learned
representation, and the design lacks a state-specific erased control that
preserves all non-epigenetic exposure effects.

### `SRC-0339`: factor-specific HSC chromatin memory

de Laval et al. provide the strongest causal chromatin result. Acute LPS produces
persistent accessibility at myeloid enhancers, improved immunity to Pseudomonas
aeruginosa, and C/EBPbeta deletion erases long-term epigenetic inscription and
gene-expression memory. This establishes division-spanning factor dependence,
but LPS training and a gram-negative bacterial challenge do not establish
transfer across unrelated families. It also lacks matched classical-state,
three-scale and full-cost comparisons.

### `SRC-0340`: reciprocal cross-pathogen HSPC transfer

Kain et al. supply the strongest unrelated-pathogen result: influenza-trained
HSPCs improve response to M. avium, and M. avium-trained HSPCs improve influenza
outcomes after transplantation. The same study reports heterogeneous responses,
rare-population expansion and enhanced metabolism. No factor-specific chromatin
write/read or erasure intervention separates chromatin from composition, lineage
bias and metabolic priming. The primary record links a 2025 erratum; this audit
therefore relies only on the reported qualitative reciprocal result and does not
promote a quantitative effect.

## Cross-study causal mismatch

The literature contains both halves of the desired anomaly, but not jointly:

- `SRC-0339` isolates a chromatin factor, without unrelated-family transfer.
- `SRC-0340` demonstrates unrelated-pathogen transfer, without isolating a
  chromatin rule from population and metabolic state.
- `SRC-0337` and `SRC-0338` demonstrate persistent heterologous response gain,
  but not operator discovery or a portable representation.

Combining claims across different interventions and organisms would be causal
composition, not evidence from one discriminating experiment. None of the four
studies supplies three scales, classical online-state controls, or full
acquisition/write/maintenance/read/replication/reset cost.

## Interpretation

Trained immunity is a real and stronger anomaly than ordinary same-antigen immune
memory. The available results are still computationally consistent with a broad
persistent gain, lineage/population bias, metabolic priming or fixed
pathway-specific state. Those mechanisms may be useful biology, but they do not
yet imply a source-identical learner that discovers reusable operators or improves
capability per full inference cost across unrelated computational tasks.

The route should not be declared biologically false. It remains event-triggered
until one experiment combines the C/EBPbeta-style factor intervention with the
reciprocal unrelated-pathogen design while matching HSPC composition, metabolism,
growth and survival.
