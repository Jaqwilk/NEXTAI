## Objective observations

### `SRC-0386` and `SRC-0394`: learned graph state, but SR or replay

Human hippocampal-entorhinal activity reflects implicitly experienced
nonspatial graphs. In the fMRI study, a weighted sum of future states akin to a
successor representation best predicts both behavior and adaptation. In the
single-neuron study, responses gradually acquire distance structure in a
repeated image graph and persist afterward, but learning covaries with
time-compressed replay of experienced trajectories. Neither study tests a new
environment family or causally separates a portable local update rule.

### `SRC-0387`: genuine transfer is the nearest anomaly

Participants trained on one hidden graph form used that prior in a new graph
with new pictures. They inferred unobserved trajectories and useful policies,
and simple or smoothed SR alone cannot explain the dependence on the previous
day's structure. This is genuine behavioral transfer and must not be dismissed.

It nevertheless fails the frozen mechanism gate. The experiments choose two
structural forms and the computational account supplies graph-derived basis
sets or hierarchical inference over those forms. The paper itself states that
behavior does not establish that the brain uses the proposed basis sets. There
is no factor-specific neural write/consolidation/read intervention, local
plasticity rule, third scale, adaptation-cost curve or full acquisition-to-use
cost. The direct software translation is spectral graph representation plus
model selection, not a new computational primitive.

### `SRC-0388` and `SRC-0389`: rapid maps are predominantly fixed coordinates

More than 15,000 recorded grid cells form stable maps after one exposure to
novel or altered virtual environments. The discriminating model attributes
that speed to fixed landmark inputs into a grid attractor; behavior changes via
downstream reward-dependent plasticity. Earlier coherent grid realignment
likewise demonstrates reusable coordinate dynamics, not an observation-written
abstract operator. Both are excluded by the prewired-grid, shared-landmark and
reward-feedback controls.

### `SRC-0390` and `SRC-0391`: nonspatial reuse retains authored axes

Hippocampal cells map a joystick-controlled sound-frequency dimension and human
fMRI shows gridlike activity over a two-dimensional conceptual feature space.
These results establish broad neural reuse, but the relevant continuous axes,
coordinates and task incentives are supplied by the experimenter. They do not
show learned structure transferred to a qualitatively different unseen family.

### `SRC-0392` and `SRC-0393`: causal schema assimilation is same-task reuse

After prolonged acquisition of a flavor-place schema, rats learn compatible
new paired associates in one trial. Prelimbic gene activation and pharmacology
provide unusually strong causal encoding and retrieval evidence. The gain,
however, remains inside the same arena and paired-associate task. Extensive
schema acquisition is uncharged, and no source-identical operator is tested on
an unrelated spatial or nonspatial family.

### `SRC-0395`: the closest software thesis is established prior art

The Tolman-Eichenbaum machine explicitly separates structural representations
from sensory identities and generalizes over related spatial and nonspatial
tasks. It is therefore a mandatory learned control, not evidence of a new
local rule. Its relational architecture and training objective are supplied,
and its globally trained recurrent computation must be charged against SR,
spectral, replay and graph baselines.

## Cross-source synthesis

The literature supports five distinct phenomena: successor-state encoding,
spectral or generative transfer of familiar graph form, fixed entorhinal
coordinates, replay-mediated within-graph learning, and rapid same-schema
assimilation. Each is important. None jointly supplies natural relational
write, factor-specific causal isolation, unchanged local transfer across
qualitatively different families, three-scale signatures and full cost.

The Mark et al. behavioral result is the only belief-relevant anomaly. It rules
out treating all transfer as plain cached SR, but it does not rule out the
registered classical alternatives. Implementing its proposed basis sets would
recreate spectral graph features and Bayesian/HMM selection; implementing TEM
would recreate a supplied dense recurrent learner. No software scoring is
justified without a new primary causal thesis that distinguishes those classes.
