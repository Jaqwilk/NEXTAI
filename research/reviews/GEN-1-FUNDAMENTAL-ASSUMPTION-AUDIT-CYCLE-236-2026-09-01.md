# Fundamental assumption audit — cycle 236

## Scope and method

This is the explicitly user-authorized `novelty-first / assumption-search`
strategic reset. It is review-only: no hypothesis, experiment plan, candidate,
benchmark, schema, dependency, runner seed or score was created. The protected
evaluator, manifest, runner and active
`heldout_repository_sequence_compression_v6` cohort are unchanged. The earlier
real-PDE authorization is superseded for strategic selection by the user's
newer instruction not to select adaptive computation or learned PDE refinement
when the causal core is already a recognizable research direction.

The audit compressed and inspected the complete 98-result ledger, all latest
records for 58 hypotheses, the 101 substantive analysis artifacts, the
183-file review
inventory, the current protocol and all 224 previously registered sources.
Candidate identity was judged by causal equivalence rather than names: two
ideas are duplicates when they expose the same information, mutate the same
kind of state, apply the same intervention and predict the same capability/cost
signature, even if their architecture vocabulary differs. Published scores
remain source claims and are not NEXTAI evidence.

The audit is broad but cannot prove that no unknown paper or principle exists.
Its decision is narrower: no candidate found in this bounded search satisfies
all of novelty, causal distance from prior art and NEXTAI, small falsifiability,
local feasibility and relevance to the full objective.

## Stage 1 — map of fundamental assumptions

### A1. Intelligence is evaluated against an externally fixed task and objective

1. **Assumed:** a task designer fixes observations, actions, targets, reward or
   likelihood before learning.
2. **Historical reason:** supervised statistics and control theory need a
   measurable loss, and benchmark comparison needs a stable contract.
3. **Families:** autoregressive models, RNN/SSM, retrieval, MoE, world models,
   RL, energy models, GNNs, program induction and continual learning all retain
   an externally auditable success functional.
4. **Removal attempts:** intrinsic motivation, empowerment, autotelic RL,
   open-ended evolution and active inference relax the supplied goal.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong and explicitly named; `SRC-0232` studies
   self-generated goals, while open-ended evolution is a mature field.
6. **NEXTAI:** active acquisition and evolutionary scouts varied search and
   selection, but every valid result necessarily used a frozen evaluator.
7. **Closed by negatives:** no. NEXTAI negatives close exact policies, not the
   possibility of endogenous goals.
8. **Necessary?:** some external criterion is necessary for scientific
   falsification, but it is not established that an intelligent system must
   internally represent the same objective.

### A2. The observation/action boundary and variable ontology already exist

1. **Assumed:** the environment supplies separable channels, events, tokens or
   objects and a declared direction between sensing and acting.
2. **Historical reason:** sensors, datasets and APIs arrive in typed fields;
   fixed interfaces make optimization and credit assignment tractable.
3. **Families:** Transformers, world models, RL, object-centric learning,
   causal representation learning, neuro-symbolic systems and active inference.
4. **Removal attempts:** sensorimotor-contingency learning, unsupervised object
   discovery, causal discovery and autopoietic/enactive models.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong. `SRC-0233` derives proto-object structure
   from anonymous sensorimotor regularities, while Slot Attention and causal
   representation learning are already registered as `SRC-0120`/`SRC-0167`.
6. **NEXTAI:** latent-causal, entity-binding, observable-auxiliary and
   cross-family contracts repeatedly tested whether anonymous observations
   identify a common ontology.
7. **Closed by negatives:** exact encoders and supplied-support variants are
   closed; the general assumption is not.
8. **Necessary?:** probably not internally, but a small benchmark that rewards
   one discovered boundary risks encoding that boundary in its generator.

### A3. Components communicate in one persistent coordinate system

1. **Assumed:** vectors, symbols or addresses can be compared after a shared
   encoder or through a globally meaningful key space.
2. **Historical reason:** arrays and parameter tensors make composition,
   batching and gradient propagation simple.
3. **Families:** dense nets, SSMs, retrieval, MoE, neural memory, GNNs,
   object slots, program representations and most world models.
4. **Removal attempts:** gauge-equivariant models, emergent communication,
   local coordinate frames and learned cellular sheaves.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong enough to define a research family.
   `SRC-0229` learns edge transport maps between node-local vector spaces.
6. **NEXTAI:** shared recurrent state, relation fragments, predictive indices,
   invariant modules and cross-family set memory all attempted anonymous
   alignment; cycle 129 identified the missing observable symmetry breaker.
7. **Closed by negatives:** the exact global-alignment learners are closed.
   Sheaf transport itself is not directly tested, but is not a novel core.
8. **Necessary?:** no; local dialects with transport are coherent, but their
   topology/interface becomes a supplied ontology unless learned and charged.

### A4. The computational substrate and primitive operator vocabulary are fixed

1. **Assumed:** training changes parameters, state or selected edges while an
   interpreter, tensor algebra, instruction set or transition law remains fixed.
2. **Historical reason:** fixed semantics make execution reliable, hardware
   efficient and programs verifiable.
3. **Families:** all ordinary neural nets, SSMs, GNNs, NCA, program synthesis,
   neuro-symbolic systems and differentiable memory.
4. **Removal attempts:** self-modifying code, artificial chemistries,
   neuroevolution, learned optimizers and meta-learned local update rules.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong. `SRC-0234` meta-learns gradient-free local
   bidirectional rules; artificial-life work explicitly studies evolving
   substrates and open-endedness.
6. **NEXTAI:** cognitive chemistry, learned VM/library, macro-DAG, population
   program selection, cellular rules and local update-law experiments cover the
   local executable forms.
7. **Closed by negatives:** exact finite mechanisms are closed; no result closes
   open-ended substrate evolution in general.
8. **Necessary?:** a physical implementation always has some meta-law. Removing
   the last fixed substrate merely moves the fixed point one level outward.

### A5. Knowledge is persistent state: parameters, records, programs or memory

1. **Assumed:** capability is carried by bits that survive between queries.
2. **Historical reason:** persistent state enables reuse and stable attribution.
3. **Families:** language models, retrieval, MoE, neural memory, world models,
   program libraries, causal graphs and continual learners.
4. **Removal attempts:** sufficient-statistic online learning, reservoir
   dynamics, lossy compression, transient computation and environment-scaffolded
   or stigmergic memory.
5. **Prior-art strength:** very strong; the alternatives still preserve
   information in a statistic, dynamics or environment.
6. **NEXTAI:** bounded state, sparse memory, recurrent state, caches, grammars,
   fast weights and predictive-state experiments directly cover these carriers.
7. **Closed by negatives:** many exact carrier/update rules are closed. The
   information-theoretic need to retain task-relevant distinctions is not.
8. **Necessary?:** persistent internal bits are not necessary if the world
   stores them, but full-system accounting then charges the external carrier.

### A6. Computation is value propagation under an externally supplied schedule

1. **Assumed:** a scheduler orders message passing, recurrence, relaxation,
   event handling or program steps.
2. **Historical reason:** deterministic schedules simplify debugging, parallel
   hardware and complexity accounting.
3. **Families:** Transformers, RNN/SSM, GNN, NCA, energy models, world models,
   adaptive-depth systems and program interpreters.
4. **Removal attempts:** asynchronous CA, event-driven/spiking systems,
   chemical reaction networks, equilibrium computation and distributed clocks.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong and old; changing a global clock to local
   events is a recognized execution model, not an unoccupied principle.
6. **NEXTAI:** event queues, polychronous binding, surprise-gated transitions,
   sparse cellular updates and energy relaxation directly tested variants.
7. **Closed by negatives:** registered event/sparse rules are closed; arbitrary
   asynchronous computation is not.
8. **Necessary?:** a total order is unnecessary, but some causal partial order
   remains necessary for reproducible physical computation.

### A7. Learning is organized by a scalar objective and assigned credit

1. **Assumed:** parameter/state changes are selected because they reduce loss,
   increase reward, fitness, likelihood or an energy surrogate.
2. **Historical reason:** scalar comparison gives a tractable selection rule.
3. **Families:** supervised models, RL, energy models, program search,
   evolution, active inference, test-time learning and learned routing.
4. **Removal attempts:** Hebbian/local predictive coding, equilibrium
   propagation, self-organization and counterexample-driven feasibility.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong. `SRC-0188` supplies equilibrium propagation;
   `SRC-0234` learns local update rules without explicit gradients.
6. **NEXTAI:** layer-local goodness, Forward-Forward-equivalent credit,
   eligibility traces, local update laws and population selection all test
   nonstandard assignment while retaining an evaluable local/global signal.
7. **Closed by negatives:** exact local rules are closed; not all non-gradient
   learning is falsified.
8. **Necessary?:** an explicit differentiable scalar is not necessary, but any
   persistent adaptation must embody some preference or viability ordering.

### A8. Learning and inference are distinct phases

1. **Assumed:** slow parameters are fitted, then mostly frozen while inference
   executes; online state is treated as a special extension.
2. **Historical reason:** it prevents leakage, stabilizes deployment and
   amortizes training.
3. **Families:** pretrained LMs, retrieval, MoE, world models, GNNs and most
   program induction; RL alternates rather than eliminating the distinction.
4. **Removal attempts:** fast weights, test-time training, meta-RL, predictive
   coding and continual/online learning.
5. **Prior-art strength:** very strong (`SRC-0105`, `SRC-0072`).
6. **NEXTAI:** nonstationary prequential cohorts, fast weights, RLS/LMS,
   recurrent residuals, learned local updates and particle state directly test it.
7. **Closed by negatives:** exact learned update rules are closed; classical
   online estimators repeatedly remain strong.
8. **Necessary?:** no, but eliminating the phase boundary does not eliminate
   acquisition/update cost or test leakage constraints.

### A9. Routing can make large knowledge cheap without changing its acquisition

1. **Assumed:** a learned key, router or sparse gate can be evaluated separately
   from the cost of building and maintaining the searchable representation.
2. **Historical reason:** asymptotic query analysis commonly treats an index as
   already built.
3. **Families:** retrieval, MoE, sparse attention, neural memory, learned indexes
   and conditional computation.
4. **Removal attempts:** full end-to-end accounting and source-identical dense,
   frozen-router and raw classical controls.
5. **Prior-art strength:** learned indexes and routing are mature (`SRC-0211`–
   `SRC-0218`).
6. **NEXTAI:** indexing, entity binding, hierarchical routing, sparse set memory,
   local experts and adaptive halting were repeatedly tested with charged fit.
7. **Closed by negatives:** the exact learned routes are closed; classical
   indexing remains viable when a usable key already exists.
8. **Necessary?:** sparse access can be real, but the key-acquisition problem is
   not optional and cannot be hidden outside the system boundary.

### A10. Knowledge growth is additive; forgetting and erasure are auxiliary

1. **Assumed:** learning normally appends or modifies state, while deletion is
   a capacity-management operation.
2. **Historical reason:** retaining evidence protects capability and avoids
   catastrophic forgetting.
3. **Families:** LMs, memory systems, retrieval, world models, continual
   learning, program libraries and caches.
4. **Removal attempts:** learned expiration, information bottlenecks, active
   forgetting and alternating generation/reduction.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong. `SRC-0225` learns memory expiry;
   `SRC-0226` alternates reasoning and erasure; `SRC-0071` formalizes relevant
   compression.
6. **NEXTAI:** bounded state, motif/grammar compression, caches, surprise gates
   and recurrent/selective memory test reduction or retention indirectly.
7. **Closed by negatives:** exact compressors and selectors are closed; a
   universal role for destructive computation is not.
8. **Necessary?:** additivity is not necessary, but erasure only helps when the
   retained statistic/certificate preserves future distinctions.

### A11. Inference commits to one current state/model rather than an equivalence class

1. **Assumed:** the system materializes one latent state, parameter vector,
   program or sampled trajectory for downstream use.
2. **Historical reason:** single-state execution is cheap and maps naturally to
   imperative hardware.
3. **Families:** deterministic neural nets, RNN/SSM, object slots, program
   induction and most deployed RL/world models.
4. **Removal attempts:** Bayesian ensembles, probabilistic circuits, version
   spaces, predictive-state representations and causal-state quotients.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong. `SRC-0227` learns the coarsest predictive
   history partition and connects it to bisimulation; `SRC-0228` unifies many
   state/history abstractions as self-predictive representations.
6. **NEXTAI:** empirical joints, Chow–Liu, SPNs, energy factors, PSR/CSSR,
   particle proposals and exact program sets cover explicit alternatives.
7. **Closed by negatives:** exact circuit/state learners are closed; maintaining
   uncertainty itself is not a new causal principle.
8. **Necessary?:** one committed state is unnecessary, but an equivalence class
   still requires a representation and operations whose full cost must be paid.

### A12. New operators arise inside a predefined language of possible operators

1. **Assumed:** a DSL, module signature, tensor layer, mutation grammar or graph
   schema defines what can be discovered.
2. **Historical reason:** unrestricted induction is unidentifiable and search is
   not computably bounded without bias.
3. **Families:** program synthesis, neuro-symbolic systems, MoE, object-centric
   modules, self-modifying nets and evolutionary approaches.
4. **Removal attempts:** library learning, anti-unification, learned interpreters,
   genetic programming and open-ended artificial life enlarge the language.
5. **Prior-art strength:** very strong; DreamCoder (`SRC-0009`) and related
   abstraction-learning work explicitly optimize this boundary.
6. **NEXTAI:** library induction, relation fragments, operator algebra,
   grammar/macro DAGs, learned VM and population programs directly cover it.
7. **Closed by negatives:** exact frozen languages are closed; no finite test can
   demonstrate absence of every external metalanguage.
8. **Necessary?:** some inductive metalanguage is unavoidable; calling it
   emergent moves rather than removes the assumption.

### A13. Numerical precision and physical execution are secondary to the algorithm

1. **Assumed:** operations of one abstract type have comparable cost, precision
   and communication semantics.
2. **Historical reason:** hardware-independent algorithms and FLOP counts enable
   portable analysis.
3. **Families:** almost all neural and symbolic systems; quantized and analog
   work are explicit exceptions.
4. **Removal attempts:** mixed precision, neuromorphic/analog computing,
   adaptive mesh/precision and hardware-aware routing.
5. **Prior-art strength:** strong. `SRC-0223` and `SRC-0224` make learned PDE
   allocation a recognizable adaptive-computation/numerical-solver direction.
6. **NEXTAI:** `CAL-20260901-0001` showed that operation counts and observed
   hardware time can differ materially; no scored candidate tested precision.
7. **Closed by negatives:** no, but the newest user criterion explicitly
   excludes learned PDE refinement/adaptive computation as a strategic core.
8. **Necessary?:** abstract homogeneity is false physically, yet exploiting its
   failure is systems co-design unless it reveals a more general computation law.

### A14. The agent/self boundary is fixed and persists through learning

1. **Assumed:** model state, environment state and communication channels are
   separated before the run.
2. **Historical reason:** security, reproducibility and causal intervention need
   an explicit boundary.
3. **Families:** RL, world models, active inference, continual learning,
   multi-agent systems and self-modifying approaches.
4. **Removal attempts:** autopoiesis, enactive AI, developmental robotics and
   sensorimotor self-modeling.
5. **Prior-art strength:** established research programs, though operational
   definitions and scalable evidence remain weak.
6. **NEXTAI:** not directly tested; every official harness deliberately fixes
   the candidate/evaluator boundary.
7. **Closed by negatives:** no.
8. **Necessary?:** not necessarily for intelligence, but allowing a candidate to
   redefine its evaluator boundary destroys the present falsification contract.

### C1 — counterfactual erasure kernel

- **Observation:** a system could erase a local distinction, probe whether a
  future capability changes, and retain only distinctions whose deletion is
  causally harmful.
- **Causal thesis:** learning is destructive necessity testing rather than
  additive storage; active state and query cost should decrease with experience.
- **Prior art:** information bottleneck, learned expiration (`SRC-0225`),
  generate/reduce reasoning (`SRC-0226`), pruning and causal ablation already
  contain the causal core.
- **Duplication risk:** it is a learned memory-retention mechanism, directly
  excluded by the prompt and overlapping NEXTAI sparse/selective memory,
  surprise gates, bounded state and grammar/cache reduction.
- **Falsification:** constant capability with shrinking active state would be
  testable, but a classical sufficient statistic, automaton minimizer or grammar
  compressor is the immediate explanation.
- **Decision:** `duplicate / named prior art`; reject as strategic reset.

### C2 — query-generated behavioral quotient

- **Observation:** represent no objects or states directly; merge histories
  only when no allowed future query/intervention distinguishes them.
- **Causal thesis:** semantics is an equivalence relation induced by possible
  behavior, not a learned coordinate vector.
- **Prior art:** causal states, predictive-state representations, bisimulation
  and self-predictive abstraction (`SRC-0227`, `SRC-0228`) state this mechanism.
- **Duplication risk:** NEXTAI already tested context trees, CSSR/PSR, empirical
  bisimulation, predictive charts and operator-equivalence indexes.
- **Falsification:** small finite systems are easy, but exact partition
  refinement or automaton minimization is the classical sufficient control.
- **Decision:** `direct causal duplicate`; reject.

### C3 — local-dialect transport without a global latent

- **Observation:** each component could maintain an incompatible local code;
  only edge-specific learned transports would make neighboring claims comparable.
- **Causal thesis:** global reasoning emerges from cycle consistency among local
  translations, never from a shared embedding.
- **Prior art:** learned cellular sheaves already learn local vector spaces and
  restriction/transport maps (`SRC-0229`); emergent communication is also mature.
- **Duplication risk:** not an exact NEXTAI implementation, but the causal idea is
  an existing research area. A supplied graph and stalk dimensions would be a
  hidden ontology, while learning them revives routing/dynamic topology.
- **Falsification:** a permutation/conjugacy fixture is possible, but positive
  behavior is also explainable by synchronization, message passing or alignment.
- **Decision:** `recognizable prior-art family`; reject as strategic reset.

### C4 — counterexample-only exclusion engine

- **Observation:** store only forbidden behaviors produced by self-generated
  falsifiers; answer from the surviving version space without fitting positive
  outputs or a differentiable loss.
- **Causal thesis:** competence grows by eliminating impossible computations.
- **Prior art:** equivalence-query learning and random counterexamples
  (`SRC-0231`), counterexample-guided synthesis/verification (`SRC-0230`),
  version spaces and constraint learning already instantiate it.
- **Duplication risk:** NEXTAI exact program search, certified bounds, constraint
  caches, MDL libraries and solver guidance cover the executable local form.
- **Falsification:** exact finite concept classes are easy, but they hand the
  learner its version-space language and teacher oracle.
- **Decision:** `direct prior art plus NEXTAI duplicate`; reject.

### C5 — endogenous self/task boundary

- **Observation:** a learner could infer which regularities constitute its own
  sensors, actions, body, goals and persistence rather than receiving those roles.
- **Causal thesis:** intelligence begins by constructing the boundary on which
  later learning and optimization are defined.
- **Prior art:** autotelic RL (`SRC-0232`), sensorimotor object discovery
  (`SRC-0233`), enactive/autopoietic AI and open-ended evolution make this a
  recognizable research program.
- **Duplication risk:** distance from scored NEXTAI mechanisms is high, but it
  violates small-test falsifiability: any local generator necessarily decides
  what counts as a viable boundary or self before the candidate runs.
- **Falsification:** no non-circular local test was found that avoids an external
  viability/goal ontology while remaining safe and measurable.
- **Decision:** `known area and not presently falsifiable`; reject.

## Stage 6 — decision

**C. No sufficiently novel falsifiable direction found.**

None of the five candidates passes all required gates. Four reduce directly to
mature causal ideas and prior NEXTAI interventions. The fifth—endogenous agent
boundary formation—is farther from NEXTAI but is already a named enactive/
autotelic/open-ended program and cannot be tested locally without smuggling the
desired boundary into the evaluator.

The earlier PDE direction is not selected. It remains a potentially useful
systems component, but under the new novelty-first rule it is learned adaptive
computation inside a numerical solver, with direct prior art in `SRC-0223` and
`SRC-0224`. Perfect success would yield a better member of a known class, not a
new computational principle.

### Confidence and uncertainty

- `0.99` that no candidate in this review may honestly be called free of direct
  causal prior art.
- `0.97` that C1, C2 and C4 duplicate already tested NEXTAI mechanism classes.
- `0.90` that C3 would require either a supplied topology or a routing/topology
  learner and therefore fails the strategic-reset gate.
- `0.85` that no safe, small, non-circular falsification of C5 exists under the
  current local evaluator boundary.
- only `0.55` that no genuinely new principle exists outside the searched
  literature and digital-computation framing; this audit is not an impossibility proof.

### Exact next discriminating experiment

None is authorized. Do not create HYP-0059, a benchmark, plan, seed or candidate
from this review. The next scientific action requires a new user-level strategic
choice: either relax “not a recognizable research area,” or expand the search
outside ordinary digital ML to a specified physical/biological substrate with a
safe falsification boundary. Until then, more architecture scoring would be an
alias sweep rather than assumption search.
