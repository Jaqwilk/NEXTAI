## Literature discriminator

Neural-process amortization replaces repeated exact probabilistic inference
with a learned context-set representation, but the new cost analysis explicitly
retains finite-representation, contamination and amortization errors. NEXTAI
has already tested the relevant set aggregation, inducing-memory and
cross-family transfer contrasts; renaming them neural processes is not new.

Discrete Neural Algorithmic Reasoning obtains guarantees by separating
predefined algorithm states from continuous flow and supervising transitions
to match a classical algorithm. Those states and transition traces are exactly
the supplied ontology hidden by earlier finite program benchmarks. The result
does not authorize a family-blind learned algorithm claim.

Learning to Condition uses solver traces to train a branching/conditioning
heuristic inside exact inference. `EXP-20260901-0036`, `0045` and `0059`
already isolate learned branch or admissible-bound guidance and show that fit,
verification and repeated-use cost can erase raw search savings. It is a direct
duplicate.

Sparse expert activation and online cascades are routing. The latter also
requires demonstrations from an external large model, forbidden here. Existing
sparse memory, conditional execution, local-expert and hierarchical-routing
results already bound the local source-identical version.

## Only retained strategic direction

Learned allocation of numerical work is materially different. Recent PDE work
learns where to refine or use higher precision, and learned multigrid work
places a trainable local component inside a residual-checked iterative solver.
The causal object is spatial computation and numerical precision under a
convergence boundary—not a representation lookup, recurrent memory or search
branch order. It offers a plausible qualitative signature: matched solution
error with computation concentrated on difficult regions, transfer to unseen
grids or coefficients, and a cost slope weaker than uniform refinement.

This direction cannot be honestly frozen in the current wake:

- no immutable licensed PDE corpus exists in the workspace;
- no conformant CG/multigrid/residual-AMR controls exist;
- current result schemas do not express solver convergence, bit precision or
  the necessary hardware transfers;
- a generated Poisson toy would encode the desired locality in the generator
  and violate the no-microbenchmark decision;
- importing a public dataset and possibly one numerical dependency changes the
  data and dependency boundary and requires explicit authorization under
  `AGENTS.md`.

The minimum credible future contract would use a public scientific PDE corpus,
three real grid scales and unseen coefficients/geometries; one source-identical
solver with learned, randomized and residual-only allocation; uniform
precision/refinement, residual AMR, CG or multigrid and privileged true-error
allocation controls; and full acquisition, fit, convergence, FLOPs,
bit-operations, transfers, latency and memory accounting. Reference solution
error must remain evaluator-private until the allocation artifact is frozen.
