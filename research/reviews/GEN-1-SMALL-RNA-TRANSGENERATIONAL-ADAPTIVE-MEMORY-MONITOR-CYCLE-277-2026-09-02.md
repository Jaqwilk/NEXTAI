## Objective observations

### `SRC-0371` and `SRC-0374`: powerful inheritance remains addressed silencing

Rechavi, Minevich and Hobert show that virus-derived siRNAs silence a Flock
House virus transgene across many generations. The inherited agents can act in
trans and be amplified without the original viral template. The informational
key is nevertheless sequence complementarity to the same viral genome.

Buckley et al. identify HRDE-1 as a germline nuclear Argonaute required for
multigenerational RNAi. HRDE-1 directs H3K9 trimethylation specifically at the
RNAi-targeted locus. These papers prove local, replicable molecular state, but
its computational form is an addressed key plus persistent locus-specific
silencing, not a rule transferred between unrelated targets.

### `SRC-0372`: natural starvation memory is broad but single-context

Starvation at the L1 stage changes endogenous small RNAs targeting nutritional
genes. The changes persist through at least F3, require RDE-4 and HRDE-1, and
coincide with longer descendant lifespan. This is stronger natural evidence
than an introduced transgene and is retained as a real anomaly.

The experiment does not train one challenge and test an unseen different one.
It measures a single starvation history, one broad nutritional program and no
matched cross-family source-identical rule. Its adaptive benefit, reset and full
biogenesis-to-readout cost are not jointly established.

### `SRC-0373`: cross-target metaplasticity controls duration, not content

Houri-Ze'evi et al. show that a new dsRNA exposure can change how long separately
initiated RNAi against unrelated genes persists. The cause is a feedback loop:
gene-specific RNAi perturbs endogenous small RNAs that regulate RNAi machinery.
Manipulating the feedback changes inherited-silencing duration.

This is the strongest nontrivial computational result in the audit. Experience
modulates a global persistence parameter shared by other memories. But each
memory's content remains an exact gene-specific address, and no unseen target is
predicted or solved better. A global adaptive forgetting-rate or gain controller
explains the signature without a portable learned representation.

### `SRC-0375`–`SRC-0377`: multiple natural inputs, evolved pairings, one output

Kaletsky et al. show that PA14 RNA P11 is necessary and sufficient for learned
avoidance through four descendant generations. P11 acts through host maco-1,
RNAi/piRNA machinery, the germline and a sensory-neuron output.

Sengupta et al. find a distinct RNA, Pv1, from natural pathogen
*P. vranovensis*. Pv1 uses the same uptake, inheritance and behavioral pathway
and targets another region of maco-1. P11 and Pv1 cross-induce avoidance. Yet
Pv1 also makes descendants avoid beneficial *P. mendocina*, which lacks Pv1 and
target homology. The cross-species effect is therefore indiscriminate output
spillover through the same avoidance switch, not evidence of accurate unseen-
family inference.

Seto et al. report a third pathogen input: PF15 Pfs1 targets VAB-1 rather than
maco-1 but feeds the same avoidance pathway. Crucially, PF15 training does not
cause worms or descendants to avoid the more distant pathogen *Serratia
marcescens*. The growing series supports multiple specificity-encoded RNA-target
pairs evolved independently, not online discovery of a reusable input mapping.

## Cross-source synthesis

The evidence separates into four computational classes:

- exact sequence-addressed, amplified silencing (`SRC-0371`, `SRC-0374`);
- one-context endogenous stress reprogramming (`SRC-0372`);
- global control of decay for otherwise addressed memories (`SRC-0373`);
- multiple evolved input-specific adapters converging on one terminal avoidance
  switch (`SRC-0375`–`SRC-0377`).

No source jointly supplies an exposure-learned source-identical mapping,
write/transmit/read separation, improved unseen-family performance without a
shared address or effector, three challenge scales and full cost. The software
equivalents are a key-value silencing table, persistent cache, adaptive decay
controller and hand-wired multi-input alarm. These classical forms explain the
observations without a transferable learned representation.

## Interpretation and confidence

Small RNAs satisfy locality, incremental writing, amplification and durable
inheritance better than most audited biological carriers. The missing property
is content-general learning: target identity comes from complementarity or an
evolved RNA-target pair, while cross-target coupling changes only persistence
or a shared terminal behavior.

- Confidence `0.995` that none of the seven sources passes the frozen gate.
- Confidence `0.995` that antiviral and nuclear RNAi are sequence-addressed,
  locus-specific memories rather than portable representations.
- Confidence `0.97` that starvation inheritance is causal natural memory but
  insufficient evidence for cross-family learning or favorable full cost.
- Confidence `0.98` that the `SRC-0373` anomaly is global metaplasticity
  equivalent to adaptive persistence control, not transfer of learned content.
- Confidence `0.99` that the three pathogen RNAs represent separately evolved
  specificity adapters converging on a common avoidance circuit.
- Confidence `0.93` that this route is exhausted absent natural cross-challenge
  gain without shared sequence address or terminal defense output.
