## Primary-source audit

### Physical Myorobotics arm — strongest near-candidate

Der and Martius ran modified DEP at 100 Hz on a physical tendon-driven
Myorobotics shoulder and arm. The same apparatus developed distinct patterns
when coupled to a pendulum, bottle, wheel and wiping setup; adding camera hand
coordinates also produced hand-eye coordination. This is real evidence that
closed-loop local plasticity can discover object-conditioned dynamical
affordances rather than merely replay a programmed trajectory.

It is one morphology with several attached objects, not two unrelated bodies.
The complete controller assumes engineered tendon-length/force channels,
position targets and a response/inverse-model mapping; the paper fixes an
empirical gain, time constants, lags and regularization for this apparatus.
There is no per-morphology disabled or shuffled extrinsic-factor ablation,
differential-Hebbian plus strong adaptive-control suite, common quantitative
useful-task criterion or acquisition-to-reuse cost comparison. Multiple tasks
on one arm cannot satisfy the frozen morphology gate.

Gate vector: `Y, N, N, N, N, N, P, Y, N`.

### Multi-body BDDHL demonstrations — simulations, not hardware

The earlier BDDHL work applies the rule form to spherical, snake and hexapod
robots, but its methods explicitly describe physically realistic simulations.
It also requires a sensor-response matrix that may be initialized from known
motor-sensor couplings, and says the empirical gain must be chosen just above
criticality. These demonstrations establish spontaneous symmetry breaking as
a phenomenon, but they do not supply the requested physical cross-morphology
causal test.

Gate vector: `N, Y, P, N, N, P, N, Y, N`.

### Short-circuit DEP reach-space analysis — diagnostic falsifier

Birrell et al. study a simulated 2-DOF arm driven by short-circuit DEP. The
environmental feedback central to the claim is removed, the inverse map is the
identity, and task solutions are selected by a grid search over 4,096 controller
matrices. The paper reports quantitative reach/circle outcomes, but explicitly
describes DEP as lacking a practical application at that point and asks whether
its apparent complexity is useful or an artifact. This cannot count as physical
adaptation or as a full-cost advantage.

Gate vector: `N, N, P, N, N, N, Y, P, N`.

## Observation

The literature contains one physical DEP system and several multi-morphology
simulations. It does not contain the conjunction frozen by the gate. Physical
evidence is restricted to one tendon arm and is confounded by its authored
sensor-effector correspondence; multi-body breadth disappears when physical
hardware is required. None of the sources supplies the factor-off controls or
full interaction-to-reuse accounting needed to distinguish extrinsic
plasticity from differential Hebbian dynamics, morphology and parameterized
adaptive control.

## Interpretation, uncertainty and decision

DEP remains a genuine demonstration of objective-free embodied
self-organization, but the active causal thesis does not generalize to two
unrelated real bodies under a source-identical, ontology-light and controlled
contract. The missing result is not a local harness defect that NEXTAI should
fill with a simulator or microbenchmark.

Confidence is `0.96` that no audited publication meets the frozen trigger and
`0.90` that the available physical evidence cannot justify a scored NEXTAI
mechanism. Residual uncertainty is bibliographic—an unindexed hardware study
could exist—not experimental ambiguity in the accepted sources.

Decision:
`CLOSE_ACTIVE_EXTRINSIC_PLASTICITY_NO_REAL_HARDWARE_CROSS_MORPHOLOGY_EVIDENCE`.
This negative closes the exact active-extrinsic-plasticity neighborhood without
changing hypothesis confidence or scientific evidence state.
