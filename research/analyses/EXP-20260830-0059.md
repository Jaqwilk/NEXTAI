# INTERPRETATION

The fixed rank-12 operator subspace does transfer enough pooled structure to outperform fitting each held-out flight independently, but it does not isolate a beneficial shared representation relative to the simpler pooled/RLS controls. Increasing training support from K=8 to K=32 improves every ARX model, yet the shared subspace remains worse than pooled/RLS, which is the opposite of the preregistered representation-transfer signature.

This falsifies the exact rank-12 learner, not all shared representation learning. The likely causal alternatives are ordinary pooled shrinkage, trajectory-specific nonlinear residuals, and closed-loop dynamics that are not well represented by one global linear operator subspace. The timeout does not invalidate the completed comparisons, but it prevents a full Pareto claim. The unstable privileged controls also make `oracle_gap_closed` unsuitable as an ideal-bound interpretation in this cohort version.

# ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS

- The runner-random anchor partition may be unusually unfavorable to the subspace; one seed cannot estimate seed variance.
- Rank 12 may be a poor fixed capacity, but changing rank after this result would be outcome-informed tuning and is not an admissible replication.
- The shared learner improves worst-flight error relative to pooled/ridge controls, so a different preregistered robust objective might favor it; the current plan did not permit replacing its conjunctive transfer criteria after seeing the result.
- The empirical-joint timeout leaves one strong probabilistic comparison unresolved, while the unstable privileged controls show that exact-condition support alone does not guarantee stable extrapolation.

# DECISION
