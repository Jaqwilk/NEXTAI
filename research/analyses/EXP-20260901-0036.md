# INTERPRETATION

The experiment isolates a genuine classical computation improvement. An
admissible possible-output bound reduced exact induction from all 256 program
evaluations to about 4--7 completed leaves and roughly 106--155 branch nodes,
without changing the optimum. Learning a global traversal order from anonymous
training supports further reduced cold search and query work relative to the
same solver under frozen and support-local ordering.

That is not a full-system advantage for the learned mechanism at the registered
reuse horizons. Exact meta-program recovery and priority-statistic fitting cost
more than the saved queries, while a fixed traversal retained identical
capability with lower R1, R4 and R16 work. The result is therefore the planned
null: branch-and-bound is worth preserving as a classical solver, but this
add-one-smoothed frequency plus single-bit unique-correct priority learner is
not a better learner per end-to-end cost.

The result does not falsify learned search policies generally. It falsifies the
exact finite rule, complete solver, statistics and R1/R4/R16 system boundary
registered here. Trying a new priority feature, cheaper meta label, larger reuse
horizon or nonuniform program distribution after seeing the result would be a
new hypothesis or tuning, not replication.

# ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS

The uniform held-out program distribution gives global meta frequencies little
stable structure; a real workload with repeated latent program motifs could
amortize a learned order more effectively. Such a distribution would be a new
benchmark claim and cannot rescue this result.

The meta role pays exhaustive inference of every training program. A cheaper
self-supervised priority target might retain the query saving, but changing the
fit target is exactly the tested causal rule and would constitute tuning.

At a reuse horizon beyond R16, fixed fit cost could eventually amortize. The
declared system boundary stopped at R16, where frozen remained cheaper; choosing
a post-result break-even horizon would tune the workload to the observation.

The missing summary fields explain the empty stored Pareto frontier. They do not
explain the direct R1/R4/R16 losses, which are present in both raw trials and
the existing aggregate summaries.
