# INTERPRETATION

The exact support-calibrated convex mixture does not discover a portable algorithmic prior. Convexity guarantees finite weights, but it does not protect multi-step rollout from a poor or unstable atom: mixing persistence with ridge and RLS can contaminate the trajectory even when the aggregate weights are legal. Training-world averaging also failed to select atom weights that transfer robustly to the two mechanical families.

This falsifies the fixed three-atom, temperature-1.0, prior-blend-0.5 mechanism. It does not falsify learned operators in general, but changing atoms, temperature, blend, calibration split or stabilization after seeing this result would be prohibited outcome-informed tuning.

# ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS

- A different seed could change magnitudes, but it would need to reverse two large DS08a shared contrasts and all six cross-family contrasts.
- The fixed atom set may be the wrong basis. Replacing it now would be a new hypothesis, not a replication or repair.
- The stability gate detects finite bounded execution, not useful numerical conditioning; the huge NRMSE exposes that distinction.
- The frozen `0.95` screen threshold makes the Pareto frontier empty for the entire cohort. This prevents any non-dominance claim but does not create the negative causal contrasts.

# DECISION

`discard` the exact support-calibrated convex algorithmic-prior mixture and mark HYP-0026 `falsified`. Do not tune or replicate it. Preserve the result as evidence that legal convex weights over individually plausible classical predictors are insufficient for robust heterogeneous multi-step transfer. This experiment provides no scaling signature and supports no claim about an LLM successor or general intelligence.
