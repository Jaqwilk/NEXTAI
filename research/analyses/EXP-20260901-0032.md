# INTERPRETATION

This is a valid negative result for the exact recursive equality-grammar rule.
Recursion was causally active: it beat the flat/frozen byte accuracy and helped
loss at small K and at one round. It nevertheless failed the central claim
because the extra confident grammar votes were miscalibrated. At K=32, public
predictions committed in earlier refinement rounds became later-round anchors;
recursive exact-match votes then amplified their errors. More refinement made
conditional loss much worse without changing accuracy or completing a span.

The result also fails the classical-null test. Actual Re-Pair delivered nearly
the same accuracy, better loss and the same zero exact-span outcome. The new
adapter was cheaper, so the frontier retained it, but this is not better
quality per matched full cost and does not establish a distinct transferable
mechanism. The empirical prior matching both causal ablations further shows
that depth-one rules supplied no usable evidence under the preregistered
two-anchor boundary.

The experiment falsifies the frozen support threshold, 64-rule batch extractor,
depth-6/length-32 eligibility, exact public-anchor alignment and
`log(1+support)*anchors/length` vote as a system. It does not falsify all grammar
induction or calibrated probabilistic grammars. Changing support, depth,
weighting, anchors, smoothing or confidence after seeing this failure would be
post-score tuning and is prohibited.

# ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS

The two-anchor rule makes depth-one expansions incapable of voting for their
own single masked byte, explaining flat/frozen equality. This behavior was
preregistered and explicitly tested, so it is a property of the tested causal
contrast rather than an infrastructure defect. Recursive expansions can vote
only near sufficiently anchored boundaries, which structurally limits exact
completion of long fully masked interiors.

The batch extractor may discover syntax fragments that transfer at K=8 but
become overconfident or conflicting as K grows. The K=32 multi-round reversal
supports that explanation, but changing support or calibration now would be
tuning. The local source corpus may favor exact program syntax differently
from natural or multimodal data; therefore the result is screening evidence,
not a broad impossibility claim.

# DECISION

Discard and falsify `HYP-0044` for this exact mechanism. Do not replicate,
promote or tune it. Preserve the implementation as diagnostic evidence that
recursive exact grammar votes can provide a small accuracy signal while
degrading calibrated loss through erroneous refinement feedback.
