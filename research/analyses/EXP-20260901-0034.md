# INTERPRETATION

The experiment cleanly separates two claims. Exact dyadic convolution of a
learned lifted linear local operator is a successful compilation technique: it
turns a sequential active-cone rollout with approximately quadratic depth work
into constant measured query work for the registered impulse queries without
changing predictions. This is a real qualitative computation signature.

It is not evidence for a better learned architecture at the registered system
boundary. The fixed quadratic observable dictionary did not deliver a
meaningful quality advantage over affine ridge, and precomputing then rebuilding
all dyadic kernels moved the cost into fit, update and state. At R16, the simple
ridge control remained more than four times cheaper at essentially matched
quality. The result is therefore the preregistered classical null: ordinary
operator exponentiation explains the inference saving, while representation
acquisition and maintenance erase the claimed end-to-end advantage.

The exact match to sequential also shows that this is not an implementation
crash or numerical-instability result. It falsifies the complete fixed system:
the 14-coordinate zero-bias dictionary, ridge `0.001`, normalized LMS `0.01`,
clip `1.5`, powers `2/4/8/16` and full rebuild after update. Changing the
dictionary, update schedule, regularization, amortization horizon or pruning
after seeing this result would be a new thesis or post-result tuning.

# ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS

The benchmark uses one sparse impulse and power-of-two depths, the friendliest
case for precomputed convolution powers. Multi-source, non-power-of-two or
nonlinear state-dependent queries could reduce the compilation advantage.

Conversely, a much larger reuse horizon could amortize the fixed dyadic build
cost. R1/R4/R16 were frozen before scoring, however, and changing the horizon
now would tune the evaluation boundary to the observed break-even rather than
test the registered claim.

The quadratic lift is orthogonally equivariant under signed channel
permutations but is not guaranteed to be closed under the hidden tanh dynamics.
A learned or larger dictionary might reduce projection error, but it would
change the tested representation and cannot rescue this exact result.

# DECISION

Discard and falsify `HYP-0046` for this exact mechanism. Do not replicate,
promote or tune the lift dimension, observable dictionary, ridge, LMS, clip,
kernel powers, rebuild policy or reuse horizon. Preserve dyadic operator
precomposition as a classical diagnostic: it achieved exact constant-depth
query work relative to its sequential control, but not a matched-quality
full-cost advantage over ridge.
